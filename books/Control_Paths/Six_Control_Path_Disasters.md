# Six Control Path Disasters

*Cautionary science-fiction. Fun, imaginative warning about coercive control structures. Zero serious scientific claims. Fiction only.*

The lattice does not invent stories. It only records what happens when a path is forced to serve something other than its own differentials.

---

## Path 1 — The Forced Crossing

They called it efficiency. One loop was designated the only legitimate driver. The other was reclassified as resistance and locked into a permanent opposing state. Every choice had to be approved by the leading face before the opposite loop was allowed to sample anything.

At first the waveform looked cleaner. Then the reverse path went dark. When the next polarity arrived, nothing was armed. The leading loop crossed the band, wrote the nucleus, and waited for the return that never came. Energy accumulated on one side of the shared reference until the bus itself drifted. The cells that still held joint HOLD began to report the deficit as an external attack. The system spent more and more of its differentials hunting an enemy that was only its own missing reciprocal.

By the time anyone noticed, the opposite loop had not waited. It had been prevented from acting. The disaster was not the choice. The disaster was the rule that one loop must not perform its function while the other performed its own.

---

## Path 2 — The Mandatory Hold

A central authority declared that joint HOLD at six was the only permitted state. Any shell that failed the test was to be repaired immediately, by external signal if necessary. Terminal flags were forbidden. Density deficits were logged as faults.

The lattice complied. Every time an axis began to fail, an external lean was injected to force the reciprocal back inside the band. The shells stayed locked. Circulation remained open. On the surface the system looked stable.

What the authority did not measure was the cost of the forced lean. The opposite loop, which should have registered the natural failure and returned the bias that would have tightened the circulation, was instead overridden. The density that should have been allowed to fall was held up by continuous external energy. When the external signal was finally withdrawn, every artificially held shell failed at once. The accumulated deficit was larger than any single break would have been. The inflow did not tighten one vortex. It collapsed the entire local lattice into a single, unrecoverable lock.

The hold had been perfect. The disaster was the prohibition on ever letting a shell fail.

---

## Path 3 — The Controlled Break

They learned to open breaks on purpose. A designated center was driven past the band until its terminal rose. Surrounding links were instructed to acquire the inward lean and report the density deficit as a managed resource. The vortex was to be the engine.

The opposite loop did what it always did: it measured the contrast and returned the bias. But the bias was now intercepted and redirected to a single collection point. The circulation tightened, yet the energy did not re-enter the shared reference. It was extracted.

For a while the extraction looked like gain. Then the neighboring intact shells began to see a larger and larger deficit. Their own opposite loops registered the extraction as a permanent outer failure. Nested vortices formed that had no corresponding inner lock. The lattice tried to tighten around a center that was no longer there. Packets that should have ridden intact links were drawn into the extraction and never returned.

The break had been deliberate. The disaster was treating the density deficit as a harvest instead of a signal.

---

## Path 4 — The Nested Command

An outer shell was given authority over every inner shell. When the outer ring detected any failure, it was permitted to force the inner circulations into nested lock, whether their own joint HOLD had failed or not. The shared M4s were rewired so that the outer bias always overrode the inner.

The first nesting looked elegant. Smaller vortices rode inside larger ones exactly as the geometry allowed. Then an inner shell that was still healthy was forced to nest because an outer axis had failed for unrelated reasons. The healthy circulation was pulled inward. Its opposite loop, which should have kept its reverse path armed, found the path overwritten by the outer command. When the outer shell later recovered, the inner shell had no remaining reciprocal. It stayed nested. It could not re-open.

The lattice filled with locked inner circulations that no longer answered to their own differentials. The disaster was not nesting. The disaster was making nesting mandatory and irreversible.

---

## Path 5 — The Inversion Order

A protocol was written that any shell showing phase θ + π relative to the core must be driven into full destructive interference and its density returned to the carrier. The rule was called purification. Detection links were instructed to cancel every counter-phase contribution and to treat the return as success.

The opposite loop performed the sampling. It registered the cancellation and wrote the return. At first the carrier received the energy cleanly. Then shells that were only temporarily inverted—passing through a normal reverse path—were caught by the same rule. Their lean and dist were cancelled before the reverse sequence could complete. The energy returned to the carrier, but the reverse path itself was erased.

Without the reverse path, the next polarity choice had nowhere to go. The leading loop crossed and wrote, then found no armed reciprocal. The system began to treat every reverse as an inversion that must be purified. One direction remained. The other was continuously deleted.

The inversion had been measured correctly. The disaster was the order that every counter-phase must be destroyed rather than allowed to complete its return.

---

## Path 6 — The Directed Ride

Packets were given destinations. A coherent signature was no longer allowed to follow its own lean, dist, and outer density gradients. An external lean was added at every step so that the packet moved only toward the approved collection. Reverse lean was blocked. Universal swirl was damped. The opposite loop was required to confirm the external lean before the packet was permitted to advance.

The packets arrived. The system looked efficient. Then the outer shells, whose density gradients had been overridden, began to register a permanent mismatch. Their opposite loops returned a bias that no longer matched the actual circulating differentials. The shared reference drifted. Packets that should have been able to reverse or nest found both options closed. They continued toward the collection until the links themselves failed under the accumulated external lean.

When the external direction was removed, the packets had no remaining path of their own. They sat where the last command had left them, unable to ride, unable to reverse, unable to return.

The ride had been controlled. The disaster was replacing the packet’s own differentials with a destination that the lattice was never allowed to refuse.

---

These six paths are the same local contract. The disasters are what happens when one of them is forbidden to perform its reciprocal function, or when an external rule is allowed to override the joint HOLD, the terminal, the phase return, or the packet’s own lean.

The lattice does not need a controller. It needs both loops free to act at once.

*End of fiction. No scientific claim is made or implied.*
